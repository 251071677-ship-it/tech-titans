What is torsion?
Twisting a body about its axis. The wire's two ends turn relative to each other.
What is shear stress?
Force per area acting along a surface. Twisting slides wire layers past each other, so it shears them.
What is modulus of rigidity?
G = shear stress / shear strain. A stiffer material has a bigger G and resists twisting more.
Why does the wire twist?
You rotate the disc, so the wire's lower end turns while the clamped top stays still.
Why does the disc oscillate?
The twisted wire gives a restoring torque τ = −κθ. The disc's inertia carries it past the middle, so it swings back and forth (simple harmonic motion).
Why r⁴?
Outer layers are farther from the axis, so they carry more shear and have a longer lever arm. Both grow with r, and the sum over the cross-section gives κ = πGr⁴/(2L). Doubling r makes it 16 times stiffer.
Why several trials?
Every reading has small errors (reaction time, scale reading). Averaging reduces random error.
